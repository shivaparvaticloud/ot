The profession

What occupational therapy is.

A registered health profession concerned with function — what you actually do across a day, and what makes that harder or easier. It is practical and collaborative rather than interpretive.

"Occupation" here doesn't mean your job. It means the things that occupy a day: getting up, getting out, getting started, eating, working, resting, sleeping. When those come apart, the cause is rarely a lack of trying — it is usually a mismatch between what the day demands and what is available to meet it.

What it addresses

Work usually falls into three connected areas.

Time and task

  • Starting when you can't begin
  • Breaking down multi-step tasks
  • Time awareness and realistic estimates
  • Switching tasks without losing the thread
  • Admin, bills and appointments that pile up

Sensory and regulation

  • Noise, light and visual load at home and work
  • Recognising overload before it arrives
  • Recovery that is planned, not collapse
  • Setting up a space you can actually work in

Rhythm and routine

  • Routines built to survive a bad week
  • Sleep–wake structure
  • Meals, laundry and the domestic baseline
  • Keeping up with work or study

What it is not

This is not psychological therapy, psychiatric care, diagnosis of a mental health condition, or crisis support. Occupational therapy sits alongside those rather than replacing them, and I will say so plainly if what you need sits outside what it can offer.

Who I help →

If you need urgent support, this practice is not an emergency service. Contact your GP, call Lifeline on 13 11 14, or attend your nearest emergency department.

This website provides general information about an occupational therapy service. It is not clinical advice and does not create a therapeutic relationship. Whether occupational therapy is appropriate for you is determined through individual assessment.
