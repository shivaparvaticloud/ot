How I think about it

What the reasoning rests on.

Occupational therapy has a body of theory behind it, and it is reasonable to ask what a practitioner is actually working from. These are the frameworks that shape how I assess and what I suggest.

Person–Environment–Occupation
Performance is the fit between you, the task and the setting. Changing the environment or the task is as legitimate a move as changing you — often more durable.
Model of Human Occupation
What you want to do, what you are in the habit of doing, and what you can currently sustain are three different things. Plans that ignore any one of them tend to fail.
Sensory processing framework
Sensory thresholds differ between people and across a day. The aim is a workable fit between your threshold and the environment you are in, not tolerance training.
CO-OP
A guided strategy-discovery approach — goal, plan, do, check — so the method transfers to problems we never sat down and worked on together.
Measured against your goals
Goals are set in your words at the start and reviewed against the same wording at the end, rather than against a general impression of progress.

Underneath all of it: difficulty with a daily task is treated as information about the fit between a person and their circumstances, not as evidence about character.
